1. Cut the leather sample into a circular specimen of 70 mm diameter using the standard press punch die cutter.
2. Condition the specimen in a standard atmosphere at 20 ± 2°C and 65 ± 2% RH for a minimum of 24 hours in accordance with ISO 2419.
3. Open the draft shield of the analytical balance, zero/tare the balance, place the dry conditioned specimen on the weighing pan, close the draft shield, and record the initial dry mass ($m_1$) to the nearest 0.001 g.
4. Fill lower Bulb A of the clean Kubelka apparatus with 75 mL of distilled water maintained at 20 ± 2°C.
5. Use a dropper or fine adjustment screw to adjust the liquid meniscus so that it aligns precisely with the 0.0 mL graduation mark on the connecting neck.
6. Mount the 70 mm circular specimen securely in Cylinder B (upper chamber) of the apparatus, ensuring the specimen remains suspended above the water level without touching it while upright.
7. Rotate the Kubelka apparatus smoothly through 90° into the horizontal position on its tilting stand, allowing the distilled water to flow from Bulb A into Cylinder B and fully submerge the leather specimen.
8. Start the laboratory timer and maintain static immersion for 30 minutes (or specified ISO test interval).
9. Once 30 minutes have elapsed, smoothly rotate the apparatus back to the vertical (0°) position.
10. Allow the specimen to drain freely for exactly 60 seconds so that non-absorbed surface water flows back into Bulb A.
11. Observe the liquid level in the graduated capillary stem using the optical zoom viewer to read the final meniscus level and record the absorbed water volume ($V_1$) to the nearest 0.1 mL.
12. Calculate the static water absorption percentage by mass ($Q$) and by volume ($P$), and compare with performance requirements.
