### Standards

- **ISO 2417 / IUP 7**: Leather — Physical and mechanical tests — Determination of static water absorption. This standard describes the Kubelka method for measuring the water uptake of all types of leather under static conditions without applied dynamic pressure.
- **ISO 2419**: Leather — Physical and mechanical tests — Sample preparation and conditioning. Specifies the standard laboratory atmosphere required before conducting physical testing on leather specimens.
- **SATRA TM180**: Determination of static water absorption of leather and footwear materials.
- **BS 3144: Method 7**: British Standard methods for sampling and physical testing of leather.

### Equipments Required

- **Kubelka Apparatus**: Precision-blown borosilicate glassware comprising:
  - Lower water reservoir bulb (Bulb A, calibrated capacity: 75 ± 2 mL).
  - Upper cylindrical test specimen chamber (Cylinder B) with integral specimen support holder.
  - Calibrated graduated connecting neck with scale graduations readable to ± 0.1 mL.
- **Tilting Stand & Pivot Clamp**: Mechanical frame allowing smooth, vibration-free rotation between 0° (vertical) and 90° (horizontal).
- **Press Die Cutter**: Standard circular punch die cutter of Ø 70.0 mm diameter.
- **Analytical Draft Shield Balance**: Laboratory balance with readability of 0.001 g (1 mg) and tare function.
- **Laboratory Stopwatch / Digital Timer**: Accurate to 0.1 s for timing 30-minute immersion and 60-second drainage.
- **Reagent**: Distilled or deionized water maintained at 20.0 ± 2.0°C.
- **Test Specimens**:
  - Chrome-Tanned Upper Leather (thickness: 1.4 mm, apparent density: 0.78 g/cm³).
  - Vegetable-Tanned Sole Leather (thickness: 4.0 mm, apparent density: 0.92 g/cm³).

### Sample Preparation

1. The test piece is cut from the official sampling location (in accordance with ISO 2418) using the circular punch die cutter to yield a disc of diameter **70.0 mm**.
2. The specimen edges must be clean-cut, sharp, and perpendicular to the leather grain and flesh faces, with no frayed fibers or delamination.
3. The specimen must be inspected to ensure it is free from mechanical blemishes, flay cuts, brand marks, and surface foreign matter.

### Conditioning

Specimens are conditioned prior to testing in accordance with **ISO 2419**:
- **Temperature**: 20.0 ± 2.0°C
- **Relative Humidity**: 65 ± 2%
- **Conditioning Duration**: Minimum 24 hours (up to 48 hours for heavy sole leather) to establish equilibrium moisture content with the surrounding atmosphere.

### Static Water Absorption Process

The Kubelka test quantifies the capillary absorption and imbibition of water into the porous 3D collagen fiber matrix under non-turbulent, static conditions:

1. **Initial Dry Mass ($m_1$)**: The conditioned specimen is weighed on the analytical balance enclosed in the draft shield and its dry mass recorded to the nearest 0.001 g.
2. **Apparatus Preparation**: 75 mL of distilled water at 20°C is poured into lower Bulb A. The liquid level is adjusted with a dropper so that the bottom of the meniscus aligns exactly with the 0.0 mL reference mark.
3. **Specimen Mounting**: The dry 70 mm disc is secured horizontally in Cylinder B. In the upright position (0°), the leather remains suspended above Bulb A with zero liquid contact.
4. **90° Immersion**: The apparatus is rotated to 90° (horizontal position). Distilled water flows instantaneously from Bulb A into Cylinder B, completely submerging the specimen.
5. **Static Immersion Period**: The specimen remains submerged for **30 minutes** (standard ISO testing intervals also include 15 min, 1 h, 2 h, and 24 h). Water penetrates through the grain, corium, and cut circumference.
6. **Drainage Stand**: The apparatus is tilted back to 0° (vertical). The sample is allowed to drain freely for exactly **60 seconds**. Surplus non-absorbed water returns to Bulb A.
7. **Volume Reading ($V_1$)**: The final meniscus on the graduated stem is read directly via the zoom viewer, indicating the net volume of water absorbed ($V_1$ in mL).

### Calculation and Formulas

The static water absorption is expressed both on a mass basis and on an apparent volume basis:

- **Apparent Specimen Volume ($V_0$)**:
  $$V_0 = \frac{m_1}{\rho} \quad (\text{cm}^3)$$
  where:
  - $m_1$ = initial dry mass of the conditioned specimen (g)
  - $\rho$ = apparent density of the leather specimen (g/cm³)

- **Static Water Absorption by Mass ($Q$)**:
  $$Q = \frac{V_1}{m_1} \times 100 \quad (\% \text{ or mL / 100 g})$$
  where:
  - $V_1$ = volume of water absorbed by the specimen (mL)
  - $m_1$ = initial conditioned mass of the specimen (g)

- **Static Water Absorption by Volume ($P$)**:
  $$P = \frac{V_1}{V_0} \times 100 = \frac{V_1 \cdot \rho}{m_1} \times 100 = Q \times \rho \quad (\% \text{ or mL / 100 cm}^3)$$
  where:
  - $V_0$ = apparent volume of the dry leather specimen (cm³)
  - $P$ expresses water uptake relative to the bulk space occupied by the leather.

### Comparative Material Behavior

- **Chrome-Tanned Upper Leather**:
  - Dry mass: 11.850 g, Thickness: 1.4 mm, Density: 0.78 g/cm³ ($V_0 = 15.19\text{ cm}^3$)
  - Absorbed Volume ($V_1$): 3.5 mL
  - Static Absorption by mass ($Q$): **29.54%**
  - Static Absorption by volume ($P$): **23.04%**
  - Characterized by moderate absorption: Chromium(III) cross-linking combined with hydrophobic fatliquors stabilizes the collagen matrix, permitting moisture vapor breathability while resisting excessive liquid pooling.

- **Vegetable-Tanned Sole Leather**:
  - Dry mass: 14.520 g, Thickness: 4.0 mm, Density: 0.92 g/cm³ ($V_0 = 15.78\text{ cm}^3$)
  - Absorbed Volume ($V_1$): 5.8 mL
  - Static Absorption by mass ($Q$): **39.94%**
  - Static Absorption by volume ($P$): **36.76%**
  - Characterized by high absorption: High loading of polyphenolic tannins and an open capillary reticular network absorb water readily. Consequently, sole leathers require impregnation with oils, waxes, or silicones to prevent waterlogged footwear in wet environments.
